1Executive summary

The problem. Twenty-six departments run their own CCTV — analog and IP, many vendors and VMS platforms, cloud or local storage, 7–15+ days’ retention — at sites up to ~1,000 km apart. The State wants one platform that reads plates, correlates them continuously with watchlists from VAHAN, SARTHI, eGujCop, AFIS and NAFIS, raises real-time alerts and traces vehicles, growing to ~80,000 cameras on existing infrastructure. VISWAS (7,000+ cameras in Phase I, 10,500+ in Phase II) and 34 Netram centres already exist; the missing piece is the connective layer.

Declared model — Hybrid. Model 1 (registry and GIS) is the foundation and the system of record for every camera. Model 3 (federation) is the spine: adapters normalise each vendor’s system into one camera contract and one event contract. Model 2 (direct connection) is used per camera or recorder, over RTSP, HLS, ONVIF or a vendor API, without changing the department’s system. PRAHARI delivers Model 4’s analytics — ANPR, watchlist correlation, cross-camera tracking — but runs them at the edge, so it does not need Model 4’s central video backbone, petabyte storage or GPU farm. Full centralisation was evaluated and rejected on bandwidth, storage, compute, cost and data-protection grounds (§3).

Core principle: move observations, not video. Video stays in the department that owns it and is recorded where it is recorded today. A PRAHARI node next to the cameras decodes and analyses the streams and sends only small structured events — a plate read is a few hundred bytes, an evidence crop about 10–30 KB — outbound to the State. Raw video crosses the network only when an authorised officer asks for a specific clip for a specific case.

The case does not rest on GPUs. A central design running only PRAHARI’s sampled analytics needs about as many GPUs as PRAHARI itself places at the Netram centres. It rests on the WAN — 160 Gbps, always on, across ~1,000 km and 33 districts, a recurring cost of about ₹60 Cr a year in the sizing model — and on ~52 PB of video storage, before a DR copy. Section 12 prices both architectures: over five years, ≈ ₹130 Cr for PRAHARI against ≈ ₹510 Cr for the most favourable central design and ≈ ₹640 Cr as usually specified (infrastructure only), or ≈ ₹870 Cr with commercial licences. Three further reasons stand independently of cost:

  • Consent. Model 4 asks 26 departments to give up operational control of systems they own and are liable for. That is a multi-year negotiation. PRAHARI asks each department only to permit a read-only client on its own network.
  • Resilience. A central-only system goes blind wherever the WAN fails, which is most likely exactly at the remote border and coastal sites the State most wants to watch.
  • Proportionality. Continuously moving and retaining video of all public spaces in one place creates a capability far beyond what investigations need. Under the Puttaswamy proportionality test and the purpose-limitation and minimisation principles of the DPDP Act 2023, the less intrusive design that meets the same operational need is the defensible one.

Model 4 is not discarded entirely: its analytics — ANPR, statewide vehicle tracking, watchlist integration — are all delivered, at the edge. Its central-recording function remains available per case, as an on-demand clip pull from the owner’s recorder (§6.6).

3.5Precedent

The pattern is established elsewhere and is adapted, not invented. The UK National ANPR Service federates plate reads — not video — from all police forces, at a reported ~90 million reads per day, through a gateway → ingest → screen → exploit pipeline whose shape PRAHARI’s central tier follows; it relies on standardised UK plates, which is why PRAHARI replaces exact matching with grammar-constrained, confusion-aware matching for Indian plates (§7). Edge appliances that dial out from existing recorders to a real-time crime centre (e.g. Fūsus in the United States) show that unifying public and private cameras does not require replacing them; PRAHARI keeps the pattern but makes the node software-only and tolerant of poor connectivity. South Korea’s district-level integrated CCTV control centres paired consolidation with a statutory privacy framework; PRAHARI pairs integration with DPDP-aligned controls by design. 4Overall architecture

4.1Architecture principles

  1. Video stays home; observations travel. Streams are decoded and analysed next to the cameras. Only events, crops and health reports cross the WAN.
  2. Department systems are untouched. PRAHARI is one more read-only stream client. Recording, retention and the existing VMS continue unchanged.
  3. Outbound-only nodes. A node opens connections to the State; the State never connects into a department network. Designed
  4. Contracts first. Three data contracts — CameraInfo, FrameSample, PlateEvent (Appendix B) — separate vendors from analytics and analytics from central services. Built
  5. Nothing hardcoded. The camera set, identifiers and URLs come from a catalogue or the registry, re-read every 60 s. Built
  6. Measure, don’t assume. Frame rate, codec, resolution, health and plate legibility are measured per camera and stored in the registry. Built
  7. Degrade, never fail. Every per-camera, per-frame and per-fetch failure is contained and counted. Built
  8. Accountability by construction. Queries on movement require purpose and case; every one is recorded in a tamper-evident log. Built
  9. Never silently empty; never hide evidence. Weak matches go to human review, trace returns ranked near-misses instead of “no result”, and physically impossible hops are flagged, not dropped (they may reveal a cloned plate). Built

5.3Catalogue-driven onboarding

For the evaluation grid, the Sentinel catalogue GET /api/ingest is the only source of truth; camera identifiers, the set of cameras and URL patterns can change. The node therefore hardcodes nothing and re-reads the catalogue every 60 seconds Built:

  • Tolerant parsing. A bare list, or a list wrapped under cameras, streams, items, data and similar keys, or an object keyed by camera id. Field aliases for id, name, department, coordinates (flat, nested under location/geo, or GeoJSON [lon, lat]), resolution (1280x720, 720p, [w, h]), frame rate (25 or "25/1") and live status. As a last resort, any string that looks like an rtsp://, .m3u8 or WHEP URL is used. The raw entry is always kept verbatim.
  • Safe reconciliation. New live cameras get a worker; cameras that disappear or go not-live are stopped; a changed URL restarts the worker. A failed catalogue fetch keeps the current camera set, and an empty catalogue must be seen twice in a row before cameras are stopped — a catalogue blip cannot take the wall down.
  • In the registry, cameras that vanish from the catalogue are marked absent, never silently deleted, so their history and evidence remain.

6Ingesting and managing streams from dispersed sites

6.1Where each kind of work runs

Everything that needs the full video — decoding, sampling, plate detection and reading, de-duplication and capability profiling — runs at the edge (or on a Netram regional node for VISWAS and for sites without local compute), because moving the video would cost ~160 Gbps (§3). Watchlist screening, alert decisions, trace, search, registry, reports and audit run centrally, where events from all departments meet and sensitive watchlists stay in one place; their cost is CPU-only and small per event. Live view and clip retrieval flow edge → regional relay → console, on request and per case only.

6.2Inside a node

  • Sampling. In full-decode mode every frame is decoded (needed for H.264/H.265 reference frames) but only up to TARGET_FPS (default 2) are converted and offered for analysis, selected by PTS. In keyframes mode only keyframes are decoded — about 0.5–1 frame per second at a small fraction of the CPU — for large, low-priority estates. Frames are rescaled to MAX_WIDTH (default 1280 px, because plates need pixels). Built
  • Latest-frame semantics. Each worker holds only its newest frame. If analysis falls behind, older frames are overwritten, not queued, so latency and memory stay bounded however many cameras are added. Built
  • Capability-aware scheduling. Cameras that the profiler has measured as unable to read plates (after at least 60 analysed frames) are still sampled, but only one frame in ten, so inference goes where plates are legible. Built
  • Disk discipline. Raw video is never written. Evidence crops are ≤ 30 KB JPEGs under a hard cap (300 MB by default in the reference build, oldest removed first); all writers stop, loudly, when free disk falls below a floor. Built

6.5Store-and-forward and delivery guarantees

  • Central side Built: ingestion is idempotent on event_id (INSERT OR IGNORE), and a batch endpoint exists for flushes. Re-sending an event after a timeout can never create a duplicate sighting or alert.
  • Node side Designed: an append-only on-disk queue with a size cap; events are acknowledged by the gateway per batch and only then removed. Eviction under a full disk removes non-hit crops first, then non-hit events oldest-first; watchlist hits are never evicted. On reconnect the backlog is replayed in time order while live traffic continues. Because events carry capture time, back-filled events land in the right place in every trace.

6.6Viewing, health and clip retrieval

  • Video wall Built: every camera as a tile showing its latest analysed frame with plate boxes drawn, refreshed about every 2 s and only while the tile is visible, with per-tile backoff for failing cameras. Filters by department, state and name.
  • Health Built: every 10 s each camera’s state (online, degraded, offline), measured fps, codec, resolution, reconnect count, decoder-warning count, last error and ANPR profile are written to the registry; the map colours cameras by health and the gap report lists offline, stale and never-seen cameras by department.
  • On-demand live view and clip pull Designed: an authorised user requests a live view or a 30-second clip around a sighting, with a case ID. The request travels down the node’s outbound channel; the node reads from the department’s recorder (or relays the live stream through the regional relay), hashes the clip at the edge and uploads it to the evidence store. The request, the approver and the hash are audited.

7.3Continuous correlation pipeline Built

  1. One event per vehicle pass. A car crossing a camera is read in several frames. The de-duplicator groups reads at one camera that are within confusion distance 1.0 of each other and within a 6 s window (by capture time), and emits the best read of the pass (grammar-valid first, then OCR × detection confidence). A reconnect or loop point closes all open groups. A “first read” mode trades read quality for lower latency.
  2. Store the event (idempotent).
  3. Screen both the grammar-corrected plate and the raw OCR text against every active watchlist entry, keeping the best distance per entry.
  4. Decide. Distance ≤ 0.30 → alert. Otherwise distance ≤ 1.10 → review (at most three review items per event). Beyond that, no hit.
  5. De-duplicate alerts. A new sighting of the same watchlist plate at the same camera within 120 s increments the open alert’s hit count instead of creating a new alert, and is linked to it.
  6. Publish the alert to every connected console (§9).

7.4Grammar-constrained decoding

An Indian plate is not an arbitrary string. prahari/common/plate_grammar.py encodes the structure Built:

  • Nine formats, e.g. SS DD AA DDDD (GJ 01 AB 1234), single-letter and three-letter series, single-digit districts, Delhi-style category letters, older plates without a series, and Bharat-series (DD BH DDDD AA).
  • A closed vocabulary of 36 current State/UT codes plus 4 historical codes still on the road, and the highest issued RTO district number per State (Gujarat enumerated exactly, GJ-01 to GJ-39, with office names).
  • Beam search (beam 64, top 5) over positions, where each character may only be replaced by a character of the class the slot requires, and only through a plausible OCR confusion. 6J01A8I234 becomes GJ01AB1234: 6J is not a State code, the sixth character must be a letter, and 6→G, 8→B, I→1 are common confusions. Each candidate carries its edits (“pos0 6→G”), so the correction is explainable.
  • The ANPR pipeline additionally tries removing a stray leading I/IN/IND read from the blue HSRP strip, and single-character deletions when a two-line split duplicates a character at the seam.

8.6Cross-camera tracking and route reconstruction Built

Given a plate, GET /api/trace/{plate} (prahari/registry/trace.py) reconstructs the vehicle’s movement:

  1. Fuzzy match. Every stored reading of every event in the time window — corrected plate, raw OCR text and each ranked candidate — is compared to the query by confusion distance; events within 0.5 are sightings. Distances are computed once per distinct string, which keeps a busy grid fast.
  2. Visits. Consecutive sightings at the same camera within 60 s collapse into one visit with first/last time, dwell and read count.
  3. Hops. For each consecutive pair of visits: time gap, straight-line distance and implied speed. Straight-line distance is a lower bound on road distance, so the implied speed is a lower bound on the real speed: if even that exceeds 150 km/h, the hop is impossible. Such hops are flagged and kept, with the reason — misread, clock skew or cloned plate — because an investigator needs to see them.
  4. Output. Ordered sightings with camera name, department, location, time (IST), match distance, confidence and evidence crop; path length; GeoJSON (route line + sighting points) for the map; the grammar verdict on the query with “did you mean” repairs.
  5. Never an unexplained empty result. With no confident sighting, ranked near-miss readings (with cameras and times) are returned and labelled weak where appropriate.

Trace requires a purpose and a case ID and is written to the audit log (§10.4).

Designed extensions. (a) Road-network travel times (OpenStreetMap graph) instead of straight lines, for tighter plausibility gates. (b) Re-ID bridging: when the plate is unreadable at a camera between two confident plate sightings, candidate vehicles there are ranked by appearance embedding, type and colour, and accepted only if the travel times on both sides are feasible; each bridged hop is labelled “Re-ID” with its confidence, never presented as a plate read. (c) Make/colour cross-check against VAHAN to surface cloned plates.

8.7Facial recognition — proportionate design Designed, not deployed

FRS is the most intrusive analytic a State can run. PRAHARI’s design treats it as an exception, not a feed-wide default:

  • Off by default on every camera. Enabled per case, for named cameras and a limited time window, only with recorded authorisation by a supervisory officer designated by the Department; the authorisation, its scope and its expiry are audited.
  • Case-scoped gallery. 1:N search only against the faces attached to that case (wanted, missing person), never against a population database. Templates are deleted when the authorisation ends unless held as case evidence.
  • Human decision. A face match is always a review item for a trained officer; it never triggers action automatically.
  • Evaluated before use. Accuracy and demographic error rates measured on representative Indian data; a data-protection impact assessment published before any deployment; periodic independent audit.

8.8Other analytics Roadmap

About two-thirds or more of the State’s cameras watch places where no plate is visible (§2.4, A6). For them, the same node runs different analytics chosen by scene class, emitting new event types on the same contract: person and vehicle detection and counting (depots, platforms), crowd density (pilgrimage centres, hospitals), intrusion and after-hours activity (godowns, PDS shops, offices), loitering. These reuse the detection runtime and the alert workflow; each is validated per scene type before it can raise alerts.

10.2Network architecture and segmentation Designed

  • Zone 1 — camera networks (department LANs/VLANs): cameras and recorders. Never routed to the WAN by PRAHARI.

  • Zone 4 — central DMZ: gateway (mTLS termination, rate limiting, WAF) and console front-end.
  • Zone 5 — central services and data: ingest, screening, trace, databases, object store, audit — reachable only from Zone 4 and the administration network; database adapters to government systems through their prescribed secure channels.

10.4Purpose-bound queries Built

GET /api/trace/{plate}, GET /api/watchlist/search and GET /api/events?plate=… refuse to run (HTTP 400, purpose_binding_required) unless both purpose and case_id are supplied. The console asks for both before a trace. The query, its parameters, the actor, the purpose, the case and the result count are written to the audit log; plates CSV exports are audited too. This makes every look-up of a person’s or vehicle’s movement attributable and reviewable (test: tests/test_registry_audit.py).

10.5Tamper-evident audit log Built

  • Each entry stores time (UTC, microseconds), actor, action, purpose, case ID, parameters (sensitive keys redacted), the previous entry’s hash and its own hash = SHA-256 over all of these (prahari/registry/audit.py). The first entry links to a genesis value of 64 zeros.
  • Database triggers reject any UPDATE or DELETE on the table; writes use an immediate transaction so the chain stays linear under concurrency.
  • GET /api/audit/verify recomputes the chain and reports the first entry whose content or link fails, or returns the current head hash. Editing, deleting or re-ordering any entry — even by someone who drops the triggers — is detected.
  • Known limit, stated: deleting the newest entries leaves a shorter chain that is still consistent. The design closes this by anchoring the head hash externally at fixed intervals (daily report countersigned by the auditor, or a timestamp service) Designed.

13Scalability, availability and disaster recovery

13.1Horizontal scaling units and load balancing

  • Edge. One EDGE-S per departmental recorder site (up to ~16 ANPR cameras at mixed frame rates plus hundreds of light-analytics cameras); one EDGE-L per ~220 ANPR streams at a Netram centre, plus N+1. A new department is added as more nodes; nothing central changes. derived §12
  • Central. Stateless pods (gateway, screening, API) scale on consumer lag, about one gateway pod per 2,500 events/s. The event bus is partitioned by camera_id, which preserves per-camera ordering and timestamp monotonicity. Events are partitioned by day in TimescaleDB; a plate-hash shard (e.g. Citus) is needed only at ~10× today’s projected rate. The object store grows by one storage host per ~160 TB usable.
  • Load. Base case 1,400 events/s average and 3,100/s peak statewide (124 million plate events a day) derived — well within the ~12,000 events/s ingest rate reported for the UK National ANPR Service.
  • Load balancing. Nodes only dial out. Each holds an ordered endpoint list — State DC gateway VIP, then its Netram relay, then the DR gateway VIP. A pair of L4 load balancers (keepalived/HAProxy, or the State DC’s own) fronts the gateway pods. Reconnection uses the same jittered exponential backoff as camera reconnection (2 s → 30 s), so a gateway restart cannot cause a reconnect storm. Camera-to-node assignment lives in the registry, so cameras of a failed node at a multi-node site move to a sibling.
